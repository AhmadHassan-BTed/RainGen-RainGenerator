01 What it is

One engine for everyone who needs weather to sound real.

Most rain sounds are recordings on a loop, or white noise through a filter. raingen simulates the weather instead: a population of drop sizes, the surfaces they hit, the wind heard through trees and wires, a lightning channel you can see, and the air the thunder crosses on its way to you. You set it in words people already use, and it never repeats.

Three things nobody else can do

400 B

A storm is a file

A 10-hour storm in 7.1.4 cinema surround is 62 GB of audio; its scene file is about 400 bytes. Share it as a link and anyone can play the exact same storm, or remix it.

Why only here: the sound is a pure function of scene, seed and engine version.

↻

Direct the weather

Move the storm, the wind, the shelter or the listener after the fact and re-render. A director's note becomes a slider move. Physics by default, cinematic on purpose, with every cheat labelled.

Why only here: a physical model plus a labelled "cinematic" layer.

c(T)

See it and hear it: the same storm

In the 4D Composer the bolt you see is the one you hear. Its thunder wavefront crosses the valley on screen and reaches your ears at the speed of sound for that air temperature.

Why only here: one stream of events drives both picture and sound.

02 Controls that speak human

Every control means something you've felt.

No bare "intensity 1.9". Rain is in millimetres per hour, wind is a Beaufort force with its land description, direction is where the wind comes from ("from your left"), thunder is the seconds you count after the flash, and where you stand is a place: a tent, a porch, a car. If a control has no audible effect, the interface says so.

      04 The 4D Environment Composer Browser prototype

      Build the world, not the knobs.

      Sliders describe weather; a world lets you direct it. The Composer is a 3D scene plus a timeline, the fourth dimension. Put a tin roof over the listener, plant an oak 12 m to the north-west, string a power line, draw the track a storm cell takes across the valley, then walk in first person and hear the physics play out from wherever you stand.

      Place objects that sound like what they are

      Roofs, windows, walls, trees (deciduous or conifer), grass, gutters, downspouts, eaves, wires, chimes, flags, cars, tents, umbrellas and water, each with a material: tin roof, glass, concrete, wood, fabric, car body, tile, gravel, soil, leaves. Sizes and positions are in metres; a wire's diameter sets its pitch.

      Storms as actors: draw their tracks

      A storm cell has a track, a speed, a radius and a flash rate. Its distance drives flash-to-bang, loudness and the rain envelope automatically, and it can be keyframed like a camera.

      Walk the world in first person

      WASD and mouse move the listener. Walk under the oak, onto the porch, into the car: shelter changes because you moved, not because you picked it from a dropdown.

      See the sound travel

      Every flash builds a fractal 3D lightning channel in world space. The same geometry draws the bolt and shapes the thunder; a translucent wavefront expands at the speed of sound for the air temperature, and you hear it the instant it reaches you.

      Animate on a timeline

      Keyframe any parameter path, such as weather.precipitation.rate_mm_h, with linear, log, hold or smooth curves, then render offline to a 24-bit WAV from the same audio graph.

      • Accessible by design

        A scene outline mirrors the viewport as a list, with distance and bearing number boxes, fully keyboard and screen-reader operable. Events are captioned, flashes are limited to 3 per second, and reduced motion swaps them for a soft glow.

      • Virtual field-recording session Planned

        A binaural head at the listener, an ORTF pair at 10 m, an ambisonic mic, a contact mic on the tin roof: one deterministic world renders every perspective at once as matched stems.

      • Paint and import the world Planned

        Brush tin, tile, glass or puddles onto surfaces; wetness darkens and dries with the rain history. Bring in glTF or OpenUSD from Blender, Unreal or Houdini, or your street from OpenStreetMap.

      • Lock to picture Planned

        Load an MP4 as a reference track, spot thunder to detected flashes, and export lightning timestamps for VFX and lighting. Stream a camera pose in over OSC for virtual production.

      The grammar film, TV and game teams already use

      Atmos panners, Sound Particles, Spat Revolution, Wwise and FMOD profilers, Sequencer and Timeline, Houdini and Blender share six ideas: a 3D viewport of objects and a listener, time as a first-class axis, an inspector for the selection, snapshots and variations, a render queue to deliverables, and interchange formats. The Composer adopts all six and maps onto how weather is designed for picture.

      1. Spot

        The supervising sound editor and director watch the cut and mark where the weather turns, where lightning lands, and what is off-screen.

        In raingen: keyframes and lightning strikes on the timeline at those moments.

      2. Block the set

        Like a previs artist blocking a shot, rebuild the location: the porch roof, the treeline, the road, how far the storm is.

        In raingen: objects with materials, at real distances.

      3. Perform to picture

        The way a Foley artist performs footsteps, ride the weather while the picture plays: swell the rain, move the listener through the door.

        In raingen: live parameter ramps; write, latch and touch automation are planned

      4. Sync to VFX

        Lightning timestamps go to VFX and the lighting desk, so the flash on screen and the thunder in the mix come from one event.

        In raingen: flash events carry the channel geometry; CSV and OSC cue export is planned

      5. Deliver & revise

        Stems go to the mix stage. When the note comes back ("storm closer, less wind"), it's a slider move and a re-render, not a new library search.

        In raingen: deterministic renders from the scene file; BWF/iXML scene embedding and ADM are planned

      Status: the Composer and its JavaScript/Web Audio engine are prototype code being written now; treat them as unverified until they pass the Phase 1 tests and listening checks. GitHub Pages can't send the security headers SharedArrayBuffer needs, so this build doesn't use it. A WebAssembly build of the native core replaces the JavaScript engine in Phase 4.

      07 Honest wellness

      What the evidence says, graded.

      Rain is comforting for a lot of people. That doesn't make it medicine. Here is what the research actually supports, graded by strength, and what the engine does about it.

      Soothing rain, in plain words: a steady, dense texture where no single drop stands out · a darker tone, more low than high · slow swells over tens of seconds, never fast or regular pulsing · no sudden, pitched or rhythmic sounds (pings, ticking drips, gurgling gutters) · a sheltered perspective (porch, tent, window) · a low volume with a fade-out timer.

      We will never say

      • Treats insomnia, anxiety, ADHD or tinnitus
      • Increases deep sleep
      • Safe all night at any level
      • Made for babies

      We will say

      • Relax and unwind
      • Part of a sleep routine
      • Mask distractions
      • Help you focus

      The wording follows the FDA General Wellness guidance. A claims linter in CI rejects disease words in presets, docs and store copy.

      "Keep it soothing"

      • Tilt −3 to −6 dB/octave, sharpness ≤ 1.5 acum, no prominent tones
      • No periodic modulation at 1 to 300 Hz; swells only below 0.25 Hz
      • Events ≤ 6 dB over the bed in Relax, ≤ 4 in Focus, ≤ 3 in Sleep
      • Thunder in soothing modes: distant, low-passed, at most one per 2 to 5 min
      • Deterministic and baked into exports; it shows a note whenever it acts
      RelaxThe default. Full ranges, soft swells, a sheltered perspective.
      SleepRain tops out at heavy, distant thunder only, a 45-minute timer with a fade.
      FocusNo thunder, steady pattern, gust swells within ±2 dB.
      ASMRClose-up detail and a Tingle ↔ Comfort control; opt-in.
      CinematicNo soothing limits; labelled cheats allowed.

      Not a medical device. raingen makes no medical claims and isn't intended to diagnose, treat or prevent any condition. A hearing-level meter (ITU-T H.870 dose), a misophonia-safe profile and a sensitive-hearing profile are planned for Phase 2b. If sleep or attention problems are affecting your life, talk to a clinician.

      08 Roadmap

      From rain generator to reference engine.

      Hero first, no big-bang port. Each phase ends with a release and an exit gate with three parts: a validity check, an adoption check, and a fallback for anything that depends on other people. Durations are re-estimated bottom-up in working weeks, for one developer and for a team of three.

      1. 0

        Phase 0: Foundations & safety

        v2.1 · solo 4–6 wk · team 2–3 wkIn progress
        • One stateful block renderer: filter state carried across blocks (kills the measured 21.5 Hz flutter), counter-based Philox random streams keyed per layer
        • The bed rebuilt as a shaped, diffuse-field-correlated texture at 48 kHz; a 1/f gust process replaces the sine wobble
        • Streaming WAV/RF64/FLAC export, a BS.1770 loudness meter, an 8× true-peak limiter and TPDF dither
        • Flash limiter (≤ 3 per second); thunder delay fixed to 2.91 s/km; unknown-licence thunder recordings removed

        Exit: a 10-hour, loudness-correct stereo render streams to disk in under 1 GB of RAM and re-renders identically; the flash test passes.

      2. 1

        Phase 1: Minimum lovable: "Physical rain" + The Umbrella

        v3.0 · solo 14–20 wk · team 6–8 wkIn progress
        • Physical rain on 4 surfaces (puddle, pavement, tin roof, umbrella or tent fabric), drop-size distributions per climate, clumpy Cox-process events
        • Wind v1 with travelling Kaimal gusts; interim thunder; stereo, binaural and ambisonic rendering
        • Listen and Fine-tune controls, My presets with share links, 10-hour export, live preview
        • Composer v0 on the web with the hero scene and "See the sound travel"

        Exit: in a blind hero pilot listeners pick the real recording ≤ 70 % of the time; ≥ 80 % of testers make it heavier, set the wind from the left, save and share it unaided in ≤ 60 s.

      3. 2a

        Phase 2a: Whole storm

        v3.5 · solo 16–24 wk · team 7–10 wk
        • Thunder synthesizer sharing its fractal channel with the visual bolt; ISO 9613-1 propagation, ground effect and refraction
        • Wind objects (tree species, wire tones), shelters: car with wipers, room with a window, tin-roof porch; storm arc and storm objects
        • Bar-exact loops and one-shots, Match my recording v1, Physics ↔ Cinematic with labelled cheats, captions

        Exit: thunder scores closer to real recordings than the legacy engine and a Fineberg-style model; "Walk in from the storm" holds up in a blind test.

      4. 2b

        Phase 2b: Wider world

        v3.7 · solo 18–26 wk · team 8–12 wk
        • A research sprint first, then hail, snow and snow hush, sleet and freezing rain
        • Sea surf, streams, rivers and waterfalls; campfire and fireplace; crickets, frogs and optional Nature packs; town rain
        • "Keep it soothing" as a deterministic governor, hearing-level meter and profiles

        Exit: every new source passes a blind pilot or ships labelled "beta" and off by default.

      5. 3

        Phase 3: Studio & delivery suite

        v4.0 "Studio" · solo 16–24 wk · team 8–12 wk
        • The Studio workspace: 4D viewport and place map, inspector, timeline and automation, A/B snapshots, keyboard alternatives for everything
        • Delivery: stems, BWF/iXML/UCS, RF64/BW64, ADM, AmbiX, captions, cue export, destination profiles
        • Creator video, spot to picture, live weather and METAR in the app, signed installers, accessibility audit

        Exit: System Usability Scale ≥ 80 for Listen; a creator makes a 10-hour render in under a minute of interaction; a post editor delivers Atmos ADM stems with timecode.

      6. 4

        Phase 4: Native core, module by module

        v4.5 · solo 24–36 wk · with a Rust developer 12–16 wk
        • Rust core with a C ABI and PyO3 wheels; stabilised models ported one at a time with differential tests against Python
        • Real-time engine with a multi-producer parameter queue and a real-time safety suite
        • A WebAssembly build replaces the JavaScript engine in the Composer and the phone web app

        Exit: the game tier meets its 99th-percentile block-time budget; determinism holds on x86_64, arm64 and wasm32.

      7. 5

        Phase 5: Plugins, games & mobile

        v5.x · solo 2–6 wk each
        • CLAP → VST3/AU → AAX → Wwise → FMOD → Unreal → Unity → mobile SDK
        • World-sampled weather in game engines: surfaces and shelter come from the level
      8. 6

        Phase 6: Signature & community

        rolling
        • Match my recording v2 (simulation-based inference); a public gallery with a remix tree
        • Signature scene packs by named sound designers; Listen-together rooms; "Find my rain"
        • The public Rain Turing Test, launched only once listeners are near chance

      Totals: v3.0 in about 3 months with a small team (4–6 months solo); through v4.0 "Studio", 7–10 months with three people or 16–23 months solo. The native core can start during 2a–3, as soon as the rain and wind models stabilise.
